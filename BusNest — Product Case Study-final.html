01 · Context & problem

It worked, but only because people held it together.

Registration, operations and maintenance ran on disconnected tools and manual handoffs, with no single source of truth.

A single bus can carry students from several institutions. That makes better use of the fleet but turns registration, seat allocation, driver assignment and maintenance into a coordination problem across campuses, without exposing one institution’s data to another.

Every handoff needed a person to transfer, verify, reconcile or communicate information.

The tools weren’t the problem. The handoffs between them were.Every handoff needed a person to transfer, verify, reconcile or chase information, and that broke down as students, buses and institutions grew.

Problem statement: there was no single source of truth for transportation operations. How might we connect registration, allocation, operations and maintenance so each person sees what they need, without anyone moving data by hand?

03 · Opportunity & principles

Not a booking tool. A transportation source of truth.

Treat the parent as the one who registers, and separate responsibility from visibility.

Digitizing registration alone would fix one part. The larger opportunity was to reduce how often people move information from one place to another.

Design for the parent, not the student

The student uses the service; the parent registers. The flow is fill in details, see availability instantly, then book or request, and students need no separate login.

Separate responsibility from visibility

Institution admins work with their own students. Central admins see across all institutions, for example seats left on Route 12.

Different versions of the truth

Role-based access and workflows give each person only what they need while the underlying data stays connected.

Connected workflow, not paper to screen

Copying a manual process onto a screen preserves its inefficiency. The goal was fewer manual handoffs.

Steps still happen in order, but they now read and write one record. Role-based views decide who sees what, so a shared bus is never one institution’s property.

Instant answer, no return visit

Availability is known the moment a parent books. If a seat exists, they book and get a confirmation email straight away. If not, they submit a request and the institution reaches out within 48 hours. Parents never have to come back to check status.

Shared-fleet seat allocation

Students from three schools and the PU college can ride one bus, with allocation tracked per seat and institution.

Routes as data

Routes, stops, transport requests, route changes, locations and bus assignments are structured, so existing infrastructure and new demand are visible together.

Digital driver records

Fuel and odometer entries move from paper registers to the phone and become a daily operational record.

Mobile-friendly PWA, nothing to install

BusNest is a progressive web app, so parents, drivers and admins use it from a phone browser or a home-screen shortcut, with no native app or app-store release to maintain.

Maintenance history

Issues, service records and status updates form each vehicle’s history with central visibility, instead of messages and waiting.

Insights

Registration trends, institution-wise bookings, seat utilization, route demand and maintenance activity are reportable.

05 · Constraints & trade-offs

Built for reliability, not elegance.

A simple Django build a small team could ship and run; tracking and parent login were deferred, and payments stay in the existing ERP.

Small team

A handful of builders covering product, design and engineering.

Limited budget

Pushed scope toward the most costly operational problems first.

Short timeline

Ship a dependable core, then evolve it.

Limited infrastructure

Favoured a simple, modular architecture.

Plain Django and a PWA over API-first and native apps

Faster to deliver and easier to run with a small team, and one codebase works on every phone. The cost is fewer device-level capabilities than a native app and no ready API for third parties. A modular structure by domain (users, institutions, registrations, routes, fleet, maintenance, reports) keeps room to grow.

No parent login in v1

Removes friction at registration and keeps the student record complete. The cost is that parents can’t yet view their booking history on demand, so the confirmation email is their record. A parent portal would fix that.

Payments stay in the existing ERP

Fees are collected through a separate ERP for now, so BusNest doesn’t integrate payments. That keeps v1 focused on registration, allocation and operations. The cost is a second system to reconcile against, and payment integration stays a future option.

Live GPS tracking deferred

Left out of v1 to protect the timeline and focus on registration, allocation and operations.

07 · Learnings

What we took away.

The real challenge was coordination, not booking.

The problem was bigger than the feature

Booking was one part of an operational system. The real product challenge was coordination.

Centralized does not mean open to all

A shared fleet needs central data with institution boundaries, a product and permissions design problem at once.

Users need their own truth

Good design gives each person what they need to do their job, while the data stays connected.

Don’t digitize the inefficiency

Move from manual coordination to connected workflow, not from paper to screen.
